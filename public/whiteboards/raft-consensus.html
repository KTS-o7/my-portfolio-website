  1. Every node starts as a follower

    This cluster has five nodes, and each one starts as a follower. A follower is passive: it only answers messages from leaders and candidates.

    Open: the “Terms and Roles” section

  2. An election timeout fires

    S1 hears no heartbeat before its election timeout runs out. It becomes a candidate, moves to term 2 and votes for itself.

    Open: the “Leader Election” section

  3. RequestVote to every peer

    The candidate asks every other node for its vote in term 2.

    Open: the “Leader Election” section

  4. A majority of votes wins

    A peer votes yes if it hasn't voted this term and the candidate's log is at least as up-to-date as its own. S1 collects 3 of 5 votes, its own included. That is a majority.

    Open: the “Leader Election” section

  5. The leader sends heartbeats

    S1 becomes leader and starts sending heartbeats right away: AppendEntries messages with no entries. As long as they keep arriving, no follower starts an election.

    Open: the “Leader Election” section

  6. When two candidates tie

    If two nodes start elections at the same moment, the votes can split so neither reaches a majority. Both time out and retry with a new term. Randomized timeouts, typically 150-300 ms, make another tie unlikely.

    Open: the “Leader Election” section

  7. A client write reaches the leader

    A client sends x=5 to the leader. The leader appends it to its own log, but the entry isn't committed yet.

    Open: the “Log Replication” section

  8. AppendEntries goes out in parallel

    The leader sends the entry to every follower at once. S2, S4 and S5 write it to their logs. S3 is unreachable, and that's fine: the leader doesn't need everyone.

    Open: the “Log Replication” section

  9. Committed once a majority has it

    The entry is on three of five logs, so the leader marks it committed, applies it to its state machine and replies to the client. Followers learn the commit index on the next heartbeat and apply it too.

    Open: the “Log Replication” section

  10. A follower with a diverged log

    Suppose S3 comes back holding a stale entry from an earlier term. Every AppendEntries carries the index and term of the entry just before the new one. S3's doesn't match, so S3 rejects the append.

    Open: the “Safety: Log Matching” section

  11. The leader repairs the follower

    The leader backs up and resends from an earlier point until it finds a common prefix, then overwrites the diverged entries. If two logs share an entry's index and term, everything before it is now identical too.

    Open: the “Safety: Log Matching” section

  12. If the leader crashes

    Heartbeats stop, an election timeout fires on a follower, and a new election starts in term 3. Nodes refuse to vote for a candidate with a less complete log, so the winner already has every committed entry. There is no repair phase.

    Open: the “Leader Failure” section

  13. Recap

    Raft splits consensus into leader election, log replication and safety. Everything goes through one leader, a majority decides, and terms expose stale leaders.

    Open: the “Where It Runs” section
